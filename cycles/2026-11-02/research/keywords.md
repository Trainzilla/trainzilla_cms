# Keyword & market research — Scale for solo → small team (2026-11-02)

Focus area: **Scale for solo → small team** (ISO week 45, `45 % 6 = 3` → item 3 in
"Rotating focus areas", `docs/feature-inventory.md`). Research done via web search
(SERP inspection). Ahrefs is not connected — see the stub table at the bottom.

## Primary keyword

**`habit tracking software for personal trainers`**
— "X software" pattern → transactional/decision intent. Dominated by vendor
marketing pages (FitBudd, HubFit, Trainerize/ABC Trainerize, Gymkee, MyPTHub,
TrainerFu, Fitsly, QuickCoach, Exercise.com) rather than independent reviews —
winnable for a product page/article with a specific, demonstrable mechanism.

## "People also ask" / related questions

Representative question patterns seen recurring across the SERPs above (habit
tracking + trainer-onboarding clusters), to use as H2/H3 and FAQ candidates:

1. What is habit tracking in personal training software?
2. Can I assign the same habit to every client at once, or only one at a time?
3. What's the difference between habit tracking and workout/diet tracking?
4. How many habits can a client track at the same time?
5. Does habit tracking sync with Apple Health or Google Fit / Health Connect?
6. Is habit tracking a separate paid add-on, or included in the base plan?
7. How do I give a second trainer access to my client roster?
8. What should a new trainer be set up with before their first session?
9. Can habits be grouped into templates I reuse across clients?

## SERP feature notes

- No dominant featured snippet on "habit tracking software for personal
  trainers" — mostly a flat list of blog/listicle results, several of them
  vendor-run "best X" roundups (FitBudd's own comparison ranks for its own
  query). Winnable with a clear, well-structured piece.
- "best habit tracking app for personal trainers" leans listicle-heavy — page
  1 is almost entirely "best of" roundups, several self-promotional.
- No video pack observed on either cluster.
- The bulk-assign / roster-wide angle is the thinnest part of the SERP: most
  results are individual vendor help-center articles, not content built to
  rank — a genuine content gap (see `competitive.md`).
