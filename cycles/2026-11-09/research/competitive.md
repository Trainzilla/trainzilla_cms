# Competitive research — cycle 2026-11-09

Focus area: Recovery-aware programming. Primary keyword: "signs a client needs
a deload week".

## Competitors ranking on page 1 for the primary cluster

1. **NASM blog** — "Overtraining: How Trainers Spot It, Prevent It, and
   Program Around It" (nasm.org/resource-center/blog/recovery/…). Angle:
   certification-body authority content aimed at trainers; covers signs,
   prevention and general programming advice. Omits any software or
   automation angle entirely — purely educational, no product tie-in.
2. **My PT Hub blog** — "Top 5 Signs of Overtraining…" (mypthub.net), a
   coaching-software vendor's content-marketing blog. Angle: ties signs to
   client injury risk and retention. Omits whether their own platform does
   anything with that data — the piece stays educational and never shows a
   product feature. Marked "Updated January 2025" — roughly 22 months stale
   against today's date.
3. **MyFitCoach help center** (intercom.help/myfitcoach) — not a blog, but the
   most relevant *product* competitor. Documents an explicit automatic
   deload-scheduling feature (every 5 training weeks by default, adjustable,
   plus a "Re-Entry Mode" after a break). Omits any top-of-funnel content
   built around the "signs of overtraining" / "when to deload" keyword
   cluster — the feature exists but isn't marketed through content that would
   rank for this research's primary keyword.
4. **AthleteData** — "AI Coach for Overtraining Prevention"
   (athletedata.health). Angle: wearable-driven (HRV/sleep) AI coaching aimed
   at an individual athlete, claims early detection ahead of symptoms (treat
   as unverified vendor marketing). Omits any coach-in-the-loop review step,
   any roster/business tooling, or an explanation of *how much* it's allowed
   to change — it's a single-user consumer tool, not coaching-practice
   software.

## Content gap — what none of them cover

1. **Content + automation in the same place.** NASM and My PT Hub have the
   educational content but no roster-wide automation behind it. MyFitCoach
   and AthleteData have automation but no content built to rank for the
   "signs of overtraining" / "when to deload" cluster. TrainZilla can own
   both: an article on the signs, backed by **Recovery signals feeding the AI
   Coach** `[GAP]`, which actually turns those signals into a plan change.
2. **A visible, bounded limit on the automatic change.** None of the four
   describe *how far* an automatic adjustment is allowed to go. TrainZilla's
   **Guardrails on AI plan changes** `[GAP]` — volume/intensity/calorie
   swings are bounded, no wild jumps — is an explicit trust/safety angle none
   of them make.
3. **A plain-language reason attached to the change, for a coach managing
   many clients at once.** AthleteData is closest on automation but is
   single-athlete; nothing in this set shows a coach a per-client,
   plain-language "why this changed" for an automated recovery-driven edit —
   TrainZilla's **"Why this changed" rationale on every AI edit** `[GAP]`
   fills that specifically.
