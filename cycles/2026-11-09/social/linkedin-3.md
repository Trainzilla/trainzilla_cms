"The AI adjusts the plan" is only reassuring if you know how far it's allowed to go.

TrainZilla bounds how much volume, intensity or calorie targets can shift in a single AI-driven edit. A rough week — flagged by recovery data, a tough check-in, a stalled lift — nudges the plan down. It doesn't rewrite the block from scratch.

That boundary is the difference between an automatic adjustment being a safety feature and being a risk. Software that's allowed to overcorrect is worse for a client than one that reacts a little slowly — and every change still waits for your one-tap approval before it reaches them.
